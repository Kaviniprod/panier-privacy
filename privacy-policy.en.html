Privacy policy — Panier

Last updated: 2026-10-04

This policy describes the Panier app for Android and for iPhone. It is a translation of the French text, Politique de confidentialité; if the two differ, the French text prevails.

Who is responsible

Panier is published by Vincent Antoine, as an individual. He is the controller of the processing described here, within the meaning of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR); "we", in this text, means him. For any question or request: contact.kavini@gmail.com.

What Panier collects

Panier asks you to sign in with a Google account. When you sign in, Google hands the app an identity token, which Panier passes to our authentication provider, Firebase Authentication, a Google service. That token carries, and Firebase keeps for your account:

  • your email address;
  • your name, as it appears on your Google account;
  • the address of your Google profile picture — a link to the image, not the image itself;
  • your Google account identifier, to which Firebase attaches an account identifier that it assigns.

This information is used to identify you when you open the app. Your name, your picture and your email address are shown on the account screen, so that you know which account is signed in. To show the picture, Panier loads it from that address, on Google's servers: like any request on the internet, this makes the IP address of your connection known to Google.

When a receipt prints the shop's SIRET number, Panier sends it to the public business directory (recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr, a French government service) to identify the shop: brand, address and contact details. If the receipt does not print one, or the registry does not know it, Panier sends the same service the brand and postcode the receipt prints, to find the shop. You can also search by brand, town or postcode: those words are sent to the same service. A town name you type, or a postcode (the one you type or the one the receipt prints), is sent to the State's commune service (geo.api.gouv.fr), to find its postcode or the centre of its map. If you open the map to choose a shop, the map background is loaded from OpenStreetMap's servers (tile.openstreetmap.org, OpenStreetMap Foundation): they receive the IP address of your connection and the tiles of the area shown — for example the town you typed or the area of the receipt's postcode — never your position, which Panier does not ask for. Like any request on the internet, each submission makes the shop being looked up known to the registry, and the IP address of your connection known to each service that receives it, with no identifier of your account. No price, no date, no photo and no position is sent.

When you scan, or type by hand, a product's barcode from its page, Panier sends it to Open Food Facts (world.openfoodfacts.org, an open database run by the association of the same name) to identify the product: brand, name and quantity. If you choose "Rechercher la fiche" (search for the product page) instead, a few words of its label on the receipt are sent to the same service's search engine (search.openfoodfacts.org), with its brand when Panier recognises it: for a store-brand product, that is the retailer's name. The page of an identified product is kept on your phone: its barcode is sent, when its page is displayed, only if no page is kept there (product unknown to Open Food Facts, or an answer that could not arrive) or if the page is 180 days old or more. The request also asks for the product's Nutri-Score and labels (organic, Label Rouge…), public data used to compare the products of a group. The page of a product in a group asks, in the same way, for the barcodes of the group's other products, and a page kept from before these two fields existed is asked for once more. Nothing more is sent: your groups, your stars and your prices stay on the phone.

Not every barcode is a product's. The barcode of a weighing label, or of a prepackaged product sold by weight, is specific to each pack: its last digits carry the price or the weight. Most of these codes are recognised by their first digits, on the phone: Panier never sends them to Open Food Facts. If you scan or type one from a product's page, the app tells you what the code may contain and does not keep it.

Other codes are numbered freely by a retailer, who uses them for products of its own brand as it might use them for its labels: their digits do not tell the two apart. If you scan or type one from a product's page, Panier shows you what the code would contain if it came from a label, and sends it only if you say it is the product's code. If it actually came from a label, the price or weight it carries would leave with it: this is the only case in which the price of one of your purchases can leave your phone.

Like any request on the internet, each submission makes the product being looked up and the IP address of your connection known to Open Food Facts, with no identifier of your account; what Open Food Facts keeps of it is governed by its own privacy policy (world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy). No date and no photo is sent, nor the shop where you bought the product, nor your location; no price either, except in the case described above where you would give a label's code as a product's. Scanning an item from the Produits tab or with the Scanner button, or typing its barcode with that button, to find one of your products, does not query Open Food Facts: the search is done on the phone, and the page of the product found then follows the rule above.

What Google's components send on their own

Panier relies on components supplied by Google. They send technical data to Google on their own, which Panier does not receive. On Android, you decide whether one of them, ML Kit, starts (see below). You cannot turn off what the others send, that of signing in, which comes with using the app.

On Android

Photographed or scanned receipts, weighing labels and barcodes are read by ML Kit, a Google component supplied by Google Play services. ML Kit does not start with the app: Panier starts it only with your agreement, and, from that agreement on, at each launch of the app. This agreement is asked the first time you use a feature that needs it (photographing or scanning a receipt, choosing an image, importing a PDF without text, scanning a barcode or a weighing label), and for as long as you have not answered: a screen then tells you what Google receives. You can withdraw or give it at any time with the "Lecture par Google ML Kit" setting on the "Compte" (Account) screen. As long as you have not agreed, Panier does not start ML Kit: ML Kit writes nothing on the phone and sends nothing to Google. (Google Play may, for its part, download the models ML Kit needs when Panier is installed; that is Google Play's doing and comes before any question Panier asks.) Without this agreement you keep the import of PDF receipts that hold text, the checking of an imported receipt, prices, comparisons, the search for a product by the words of its label, and typing a barcode by hand, which asks for nothing and does not use ML Kit. What reads a photo, a PDF without text, a weighing label or a barcode with the camera needs ML Kit: the feature then tells you so and offers to let you allow it (except when choosing the shop of an already saved receipt: without your agreement, Panier then does not re-read the photo and asks you nothing; the shop-choice screen opens with no suggestion).

Once ML Kit has started, reading is done on the phone: according to ML Kit's terms of use, neither the images, nor the text, nor the codes read are sent to Google. ML Kit does, however, send Google diagnostic and usage data, including: the phone's make (its manufacturer) and model, its Android version and build number, and the compute accelerators it has; the languages and region set on the phone, its time zone, the type of network (Wi-Fi or mobile) and the SIM card's operator code; the app's name and version; processing times; the settings used, such as the format and resolution of images; the size of the data processed; for a barcode, its format and type, never its value; the type of operation (start-up, model download, reading, use of the scanner) and error codes. Two identifiers go with them: a device identifier, whose nature Google does not specify, and an identifier specific to the installation of Panier, which ML Kit writes on the phone only once it has started. These data leave over an encrypted connection, sometimes deferred: the phone may send them while Panier is closed. Like any request on the internet, these submissions also make the IP address of your connection known to Google. Google uses these data for its own purposes (diagnostics, usage statistics, improving ML Kit, abuse detection), under its privacy rules (policies.google.com/privacy). ML Kit itself offers no setting to stop them: this is why Panier starts it only with your agreement.

If you withdraw your agreement, Panier records it at once: ML Kit is no longer used for a new reading. Panier then cancels the submissions ML Kit had scheduled and erases its queue and its settings on the phone, identifier included, but only when the app starts, before ML Kit is touched. This erasing of what has not yet left is complete when Panier is next started from scratch (after it was fully closed, or after the phone restarted); if the app is reopened right away, without having been fully closed, part of it may wait for that next start. Until then, what was waiting may still leave. What Google has already received cannot be taken back, nor what Google Play services hold on their side. Your choice is kept on the phone, in one small value that Panier sends nowhere.

When you sign in, and then each time the app opens, the requests sent to Firebase Authentication make known to Google, in addition to the account data described above, the IP address of your connection and technical information: the Android version; the phone's product name, model and brand, as set by its manufacturer, and its type (phone, watch, television…); the app that installed Panier (for example the Play Store); the version of the Firebase components. Google states that it does not attach this information to any user or device identifier. The same requests also state the phone's language and the previous days on which Panier was opened. Google uses all of it to secure authentication and prevent abuse, and to track the adoption of its components; it states that it keeps IP addresses for a few weeks.

On iPhone

Receipts, weighing labels and barcodes are read by Apple's components (Vision and VisionKit), not by a Google component: none of them sends diagnostic data to Google for this reading, and Panier sends what is read nowhere. What Apple processes on its side is governed by its own privacy policy. Panier does not ask for your agreement for this reading and offers no setting: there is no ML Kit on iPhone.

When you sign in, and then each time the app opens, the requests sent to Firebase Authentication make known to Google, in addition to the account data described above, the IP address of your connection and technical information: the iOS version; the phone's model, given by its hardware identifier (of the kind "iPhone17,1"); whether Panier was installed from the App Store; the app's name (com.panier.panier) and version; the versions of the Firebase components and of the tool that built the app; the app's language, French, whatever the phone's language. Google states that it does not attach this information to any user or device identifier. The same requests also state the days on which Panier came to the foreground since the previous request (at most thirty days are kept), each with this technical information. Google uses all of it to secure authentication and prevent abuse, and to track the adoption of its components; it states that it keeps IP addresses for a few weeks.

Signing in itself takes place in the system's sign-in window, on accounts.google.com. Google's sign-in component adds to its requests the iOS version, its own version and the app's identifier. Google states that it may collect, on its side, a user identifier and the IP address (policies.google.com/privacy); Panier does not see them.

To our knowledge, no Google component in Panier reads, on the iPhone, its advertising identifier, the name you gave it, or an identifier specific to the installation of Panier.

On both

Panier holds none of this technical data: it can neither give it to you nor erase it (apart from what ML Kit has queued on the phone to send, which Panier erases when you withdraw your agreement, as said above), and deleting your Panier account does not erase it. To exercise your rights over this data, you can contact Google (policies.google.com/privacy).

What Panier does not collect

Your receipts, the photographs you take of them, the prices and your purchase history stay on your phone. They are sent neither to us nor to a third party; of shops and products, only the items described above leave, with the one reservation made there for the code of a label that you would say is a product's. Panier does not collect your position.

Panier also keeps them out of the phone's automatic backups, which would otherwise copy your history: to Google on Android, to Apple (iCloud) or to your computer on iPhone. On Android, only the direct transfer from one phone to another, which Android offers when you change device, can carry your history to the new one; photographs do not follow. On iPhone, Panier keeps its history in a folder that it asks iOS to exclude from backups. Apple presents this request as an indication given to the system, not as a guarantee. Consequence: an iPhone restored from a backup, or replaced, starts without your history.

The photograph or PDF of a receipt is kept in the app's private storage: this file is erased with its receipt, and with your account. The system (Android or iOS) may also erase it on its own when the phone is short of space; the receipt and its prices are not affected.

One exception: if the app is interrupted while it is reading a receipt or before it is saved, a copy of its file may remain in the app's storage. Depending on the case, it is erased the next time Panier opens, with your account, or when the system empties the app's cache; at the latest, when the app is uninstalled.

Why, and on what legal basis

What Panier itself sends serves three purposes:

  • to identify you as a contributor. Panier is preparing a price database shared among the users of the same area. It does not exist yet: today, no price is shared. Such a database needs contributors with a stable, verifiable identity, to attribute readings, moderate them and resist abuse; this is why the account is required from now on. In the meantime, it serves to recognise you when the app opens and to keep each account's data separate on the phone;
  • to identify a receipt's shop, to compare prices shop by shop;
  • to identify a product, to know its brand, name and quantity.

The first processing rests on our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) in admitting only identified contributors to that future database. The other two are necessary for the performance of the contract between us as soon as you use the app (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR): they provide the functions you use in it.

Signing in with a Google account is mandatory: without it, Panier does not open. The SIRET number of a receipt that prints one is sent when the receipt is read, without your having to ask; likewise the brand and postcode a receipt prints are sent when no SIRET identifies the shop. Identifying a product is optional: nothing is sent to Open Food Facts until you have scanned or typed a product's barcode or asked for its page.

Panier makes no automated decision about you and builds no profile.

Recipients

  • Google, for Firebase Authentication: it keeps your account data for us and on our instructions, as a processor. Firebase Authentication operates exclusively in the United States: your account data is therefore transferred there. This transfer is covered by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, which the European Commission found adequate on 10 July 2023 and to which Google adheres; failing that, by the Commission's standard contractual clauses, provided for in the contract between us and Google and a copy of which you can obtain by writing to us. What its components send on their own, Google processes for its own purposes, as stated above.
  • GitHub, which hosts this policy, the account deletion page and the home page (GitHub Pages): like any host, it receives the IP address of the connection of whoever views them, under its own responsibility.
  • The French State (recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr and geo.api.gouv.fr), the association Open Food Facts and the OpenStreetMap Foundation (tile.openstreetmap.org): they receive the requests described above, with the IP address of your connection, and process them under their own responsibility.

No data is sold or passed to recipients other than those this policy names.

Retention and deletion

Firebase keeps your account data — email address, name, picture address and identifiers — as long as the account exists.

What Panier keeps on your phone stays there until you delete it: a receipt, with its photograph, from its page; everything, with your account or by uninstalling the app.

The business directory and Open Food Facts keep the requests they receive under their own rules; Open Food Facts states that it keeps its connection logs for three years.

You can delete your account at any time from the app: the account icon, at the top right of any of the four tabs (Panier, Évolutions, Produits, Tickets) → Supprimer mon compte ("Delete my account"; the app is in French). Deletion removes your account from Firebase Authentication and erases from your phone your history, your receipts and their photographs. It is final: the account ceases to exist at once, and Google finishes erasing its data from its systems, backups included, within 180 days at most.

Without the app, you can delete it from the account deletion page: you sign in with Google, see the account's address, then confirm; deletion is immediate. This page loads Firebase's kit from Google's servers (www.gstatic.com) and opens Google's sign-in window: like any request on the internet, this makes the IP address of your connection known to Google. It does not keep your session in the browser: it ends with the tab. Firebase's kit only records there, as in the app, a log of the days it was used (the date and the kit's versions), which it attaches to its requests to Google. It deletes only the account: what Panier keeps on your phone is erased by uninstalling the app.

You can also write to contact.kavini@gmail.com; the request is handled within one month.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, you can ask for access to your data, its rectification, its erasure, the restriction of its processing and its portability, by writing to contact.kavini@gmail.com. You can also object to the processing that rests on our legitimate interest, that of your account: as Panier does not open without an account, this amounts to deleting it. We reply within one month.

These requests concern your account data, the only data we hold. Your receipts, your prices and your photographs are only on your phone: it is in the app that you view, correct and delete them.

You can also, at any time, lodge a complaint with the CNIL (www.cnil.fr), the French data protection authority.
